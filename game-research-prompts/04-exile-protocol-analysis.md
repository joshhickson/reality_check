# Research Prompt 4: "Exile Protocol" Catch-Up Mechanic Analysis

## 1. Objective

To conduct a deep analysis of the "Exile Protocol" mechanic, evaluating its potential impact on player experience, game balance, and group dynamics. This analysis should be framed by established best practices for catch-up mechanics in multiplayer games. The goal is to identify potential risks and provide concrete recommendations for refinement or replacement.

## 2. Context

The "Exile Protocol" is a player-driven catch-up mechanic designed to address a runaway leader. It does this by punishing the leading player and fundamentally changing their victory condition, turning them into a disruptive force. While innovative, this type of direct, punitive mechanic can be controversial and risks creating negative player experiences (so-called "feelbad" moments) if not implemented carefully. It is currently detailed in the GDD but is absent from the Player Manual, suggesting its status may be uncertain.

## 3. Provided Document: GDD Excerpt (The Exile Protocol)

```markdown
### 4.1. The Exile Protocol (Replaces Red Line Protocol)

**Concept:** A player-driven catch-up mechanism that addresses a runaway leader without resorting to player elimination. It transforms a dominant player into a new kind of disruptive force, keeping all players engaged.

**Rules:**
*   **Trigger Condition:** The Exile Protocol can be initiated at the start of any player's turn if one player's **Money** is more than double the combined total of the two players with the least Money.
*   **The Vote:** The player whose turn it is may propose a vote to "Exile" the wealthy player. The vote requires a simple majority to pass. The wealthy player does not get a vote.
*   **Consequences of Exile:**
    *   The Exiled player immediately loses 50% of their Money, which is redistributed equally among all other players.
    *   The Exiled player receives a new secret objective card (e.g., "Cause two other players to fall below 3 Mental Health," or "Ensure the player with the highest Virtue does not win.").
    *   The Exiled player no longer pursues the standard victory conditions. They can only win by achieving their new secret objective. They are still part of the game, but their goals are now fundamentally different and often disruptive.
```

## 4. Research Questions

Please provide a detailed analysis answering the following questions:

1.  **Analysis vs. Best Practices:**
    *   How does the "Exile Protocol" compare to industry best practices for catch-up mechanics?
    *   Does it fall into the category of a "feelbad" or punitive mechanic? Explain why or why not.
    *   Analyze the mechanic's trigger condition. Is it clear, fair, and easy to calculate? Does it target the "correct" definition of a runaway leader in this game (i.e., is Money the only important factor)?

2.  **Player Experience & Psychological Impact:**
    *   Describe the likely emotional experience of the player who gets "Exiled." Does this new role as a "disruptive force" sound fun and engaging, or frustrating and isolating?
    *   Describe the likely experience of the *other* players. Does the protocol empower them, or does it simply formalize a "mob mentality"?
    *   How does this mechanic serve the game's core vision of creating "memorable stories"? What kinds of stories is it likely to create?

3.  **Risk Assessment & Mitigation:**
    *   What is the single biggest risk associated with implementing this mechanic as written? (e.g., Kingmaking, players feeling targeted, social friction outside the game).
    *   Could the "Exile Protocol" be exploited? For example, could players collude to trigger it strategically against a player who is not actually the biggest threat?
    *   How might this mechanic interact with other game systems, such as the "Peer Testimony" phase at the end of the game?

4.  **Actionable Recommendations for Improvement:**
    *   Suggest at least **two specific, concrete modifications** to the existing "Exile Protocol" that would mitigate its risks while preserving its intended purpose. (e.g., Change the trigger, modify the consequences, give the Exiled player more agency, etc.).
    *   Propose **one alternative catch-up mechanic** that is less punitive but still helps trailing players stay competitive. This alternative should be inspired by best practices (e.g., a "big move" opportunity, a subtle "ramping" mechanic).
    *   What is your final recommendation? Should the team **(a) Keep** the protocol as is, **(b) Refine** it based on your suggestions, or **(c) Replace** it with your proposed alternative? Justify your final answer.
