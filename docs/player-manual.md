# Reality Check: The Official Player Manual

## 1. Introduction: Welcome to Babel

> "We have to laugh at life in order to see it as it truly is."

Welcome to **Reality Check**, the game of modern survival, sin, and satire. Set in the fictional, hyper-modern city of Babel, you will navigate the beautiful chaos of adult life. From the soul-crushing demands of your career to the fleeting joys of a viral social media post, this game mirrors the absurdities and anxieties of our time.

You won't be slaying dragons or conquering galaxies. Instead, you'll be trying to pay your rent, keep your therapist happy, and decide whether that tempting but morally bankrupt opportunity is *really* worth it. Your choices matter, shaping not only your own destiny but the very fabric of the community around you.

Are you ready for your Reality Check?

## 2. The Objective: What is "Winning"?

In Reality Check, "winning" is as complicated as it is in real life. There is no single path to victory. At the end of the game, all players face **Judgment Day**, a final reckoning of the life they've lived.

You can win by:

*   **Achieving the Highest Score:** A raw calculation of your life's successes and failures.
*   **Becoming The Saint:** Ending the game with the highest Virtue and lowest Sin.
*   **Becoming The Hustler:** Amassing the most Money without completely selling your soul.
*   **Becoming The Survivor:** Finishing with the highest Mental Health, proving your resilience against the chaos.
*   **Becoming The Builder:** Having the most positive impact on the lives of the other players.

The "best" victory is for you and your friends to decide. What kind of life do you value most?

## 3. Game Components

### The Chaos Circle
The game board is a unique, mathematically generated set of nested rings, each representing a different domain of your life:

*   **Career Ring (Red):** The relentless world of jobs, promotions, and corporate drama.
*   **Health Ring (Gold):** The delicate balance of your physical and mental wellbeing.
*   **Social Ring (Cyan):** The intricate web of relationships, family, and community.
*   **Personal Growth Ring (Green):** The search for meaning through hobbies, spirituality, and side hustles.
*   **Babel Events Ring (Purple):** The unpredictable, town-wide crises that affect all players.

### The Card Decks
The narrative of the game is driven by three distinct decks of cards:

*   **Sin Cards:** Temptations that offer immediate, often selfish, benefits at a moral or long-term cost.
*   **Virtue Cards:** Opportunities for self-sacrifice and moral growth, often requiring a personal cost for a collective benefit.
*   **Chaos Cards:** The random, uncontrollable events of life—from unexpected windfalls to mysterious illnesses.

### The Four Pillars of Life (Your Stats)
Every player must manage four core stats:

*   **💰 Money:** Your financial health. Necessary for survival and opening up certain opportunities.
*   **🧠 Mental Health:** Your psychological wellbeing. A low score can lead to burnout and poor decisions.
*   **☠️ Sin:** A measure of your moral corruption. High sin can lead to dire consequences.
*   **✝️ Virtue:** A measure of your moral strength and altruism. High virtue unlocks unique social opportunities.

## 4. Game Setup

1.  **Gather Your Friends:** The game is designed for 3-5 players.
2.  **Join the Game:** All players connect to the game session.
3.  **Receive Your Identity:** Each player is assigned a random starting character, complete with unique traits and a potential drawback that will influence their journey.
4.  **Starting Stats:** All players begin with a baseline of Money, Mental Health, Sin, and Virtue.
5.  **The First Turn:** The first player is chosen at random, and the game begins!

## 5. Gameplay: On Your Turn

A game of Reality Check consists of 10 rounds. On your turn, you will perform the following actions in order:

1.  **Apply Ongoing Effects:** Any persistent effects from cards you've played on previous turns will trigger at the start of your turn. This could be anything from a monthly bill to a lingering feeling of dread.
2.  **Take Your Action:** You have a choice of one of the following actions:
    *   **Draw a Card:** Draw a card from the deck. This is the most common action. The card will present you with a scenario and a choice.
    *   **Play a Card:** Play a card from your hand and resolve one of its choices.
3.  **Resolve the Action:**
    *   If you drew a card, you must read it aloud and make a choice. The effects of your choice are applied immediately to your stats.
    *   If you played a card, the effects of your chosen action are applied.
4.  **End Your Turn:** Once your action is resolved, your turn is over. Play proceeds to the next player in clockwise order.

## 6. Advanced Rules & Future Plans

The world of Babel is ever-expanding. The following mechanics are part of the advanced game design and may be introduced in future updates.

### The Control Chain System
*   **Corporate Ladders:** Players may eventually be able to "employ" other players, creating complex power dynamics and dependencies.
*   **Political Influence:** Use your wealth and clout to influence community-wide decisions, for good or for ill.

### The Red Line Protocol
*   In games with extreme inequality, players may be given the option to vote to "eliminate" a player who has become too powerful, redistributing their wealth but at a great moral cost to the community.

### Systemic Inequality
*   The "Birth Lottery" may be expanded to include factors like family wealth, educational background, and inherited social capital, creating more realistic starting advantages and disadvantages.

### Expansions
*   Future expansions are planned to explore different life stages, such as the **College Years**, the **Midlife Crisis**, and **Retirement**, each with unique mechanics and challenges.

## 7. End of Game: Judgment Day

After the final round, the game ends and Judgment Day begins.

1.  **Final Scoring:** Your final score is calculated based on your stats, with bonuses for living a balanced life and having a positive impact on your community.
2.  **Peer Testimony:** Players have the opportunity to comment on each other's journeys, commending acts of virtue and raising concerns about acts of sin.
3.  **Victory:** The player with the highest score is the "winner," but the group also determines winners for the other victory conditions (The Saint, The Hustler, The Survivor, The Builder).

The ultimate goal is to spark conversation. Who played the "best" game? What does that even mean? That's your final Reality Check.
