# Playtesting Plan & Scenarios

*This document outlines a focused playtesting strategy for "Reality Check," designed to gather empirical feedback on the high-risk mechanics identified in the GDD review. The goal is to move from theoretical balancing to iterative, player-centric design.*

## Guiding Principles

*   **Test Early, Test Often:** Prototypes do not need to be perfect. Even a simple paper prototype can provide invaluable feedback.
*   **Focus on Feelings, Not Just Bugs:** The primary goal of these tests is to gather qualitative feedback on the player experience. Ask players how specific moments *felt*, not just if the rules "worked."
*   **Diverse Groups:** Test with a mix of player types, including the target "Social Gamer" demographic and more analytical "Light Strategist" players.

## Test Scenario A: The "Punishment Spiral"

*   **Objective:** To assess the experiential impact of the "Burnout" mechanic and determine if the "Crossroads Immunity" revision is sufficient to prevent player disengagement.
*   **Setup:**
    1.  Use a 4-player setup.
    2.  During the first few rounds, artificially force one player (the "Test Subject") into a "Burnout" state (Mental Health <= 3). This can be done by having other players target them or by rigging the initial card draws.
    3.  Ensure the "Crossroads" deck is stacked with several negative-effect cards.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Observe the Test Subject's engagement levels over the next 3-4 rounds.
    2.  Pay close attention to their choices and their verbal and non-verbal reactions.
*   **Key Questions for Post-Game Debrief:**
    *   (To the Test Subject): Did you feel like you had a chance to recover?
    *   (To the Test Subject): At what point, if any, did you feel like you were "out of the game"?
    *   (To all players): Did the "Crossroads Immunity" for the Burnout player feel fair?

## Test Scenario B: The "Kingmaker" Effect

*   **Objective:** To evaluate the revised "Exile Protocol" and determine if it successfully mitigates the "kingmaker" problem while still functioning as a valid catch-up mechanic.
*   **Setup:**
    1.  Use a 4-player setup.
    2.  Intentionally manipulate the game state so that one player becomes a runaway financial leader, triggering the "Exile Protocol."
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Have the players vote to Exile the leader.
    2.  Give the now-Exiled player their new "Secret Comeback Objective."
    3.  Observe the subsequent social dynamics and the Exiled player's strategic choices.
*   **Key Questions for Post-Game Debrief:**
    *   (To the Exiled player): Did your new objective feel achievable? Did it feel more engaging than simply trying to spoil someone else's game?
    *   (To the other players): Did the Exiled player's actions feel fair? Did their presence still feel meaningful to the game's outcome?
    *   Did the game's conclusion feel satisfying and earned, or was it arbitrary?

## Test Scenario C: Cognitive Load & Scoring Clarity

*   **Objective:** To measure the cognitive load imposed by the game's economy and assess the intuitiveness of the simplified scoring formula.
*   **Setup:**
    1.  Use a group of 3-4 players who have **never seen or played the game before.**
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Explain the rules as you would to any new player.
    2.  Observe their decision-making process during the game. Note any instances of "Analysis Paralysis," where a player takes an excessively long time to decide on an action.
    *   Pay attention to how often players ask for clarification on the scoring rules.
*   **Key Questions for Post-Game Debrief:**
    *   (To all players): Could you explain the final scoring formula in your own words?
    *   (To all players): When you made a choice (e.g., taking a Sin point for some Money), did you feel like you had a good grasp of the long-term scoring impact?
    *   What was the most confusing part of the game?
    *   Were there any moments where you felt overwhelmed by the number of things to track? If so, what were they?
