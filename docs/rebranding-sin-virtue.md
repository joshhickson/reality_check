# Game Design Proposal: Rebranding "Sin" and "Virtue"

## 1. Executive Summary

The terms "Sin" and "Virtue" are powerful, but they carry a weight that feels slightly misaligned with the satirical, modern tone of "Reality Check." For the target audience of 21-35 year-olds, these words can sound archaic, overly dramatic, or even preachy.

To enhance the game's humor and relatability, I propose rebranding these two core stats to something that better reflects the everyday moral trade-offs of modern life. This document outlines the rationale for this change and provides several alternative naming conventions.

## 2. Rationale for Change

*   **Tone-Deafness:** The current terms can feel out of place in a game that satirizes things like influencer culture and corporate jargon.
*   **Lack of Nuance:** "Sin" implies a level of evil that doesn't quite fit actions like "insider trading" or "embezzlement," which are often framed in more sterile, corporate terms.
*   **Relatability:** The target audience is more likely to think in terms of "selling out," "hustling," or "self-care" than in terms of "sin" and "virtue."

By updating these terms, we can make the game's core conflict feel more immediate and relevant to the players' own lives.

## 3. Proposed Naming Conventions

Here are three potential rebranding options, each with a slightly different flavor.

### Option 1: "Shortcut" vs. "Sacrifice"

This option focuses on the *process* of the choices.

| Old Name | New Name | Symbol | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sin | **Shortcut** | ✂️ | Implies cutting corners, taking the easy way out, and a lack of integrity. It's less about being "evil" and more about being lazy or opportunistic. |
| Virtue | **Sacrifice** | 🙏 | Highlights the personal cost of making the "right" choice. It's about giving something up for a greater good or a long-term benefit. |

**Why it works:** This pairing is morally neutral and focuses on the mechanics of the choice. It's a very modern way of looking at success and morality.

### Option 2: "Sellout" vs. "Self-Care"

This option focuses on the *personal impact* of the choices.

| Old Name | New Name | Symbol | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sin | **Sellout** | 💲 | A highly relatable term for the target audience. It captures the feeling of compromising one's values for money or fame. |
| Virtue | **Self-Care** | ❤️ | A modern, almost buzzword-y term that can be used both sincerely and ironically. It represents prioritizing one's own well-being and principles. |

**Why it works:** This pairing is dripping with satire. It uses the language of the target audience to critique the very culture it's a part of.

### Option 3: "Hustle" vs. "Help"

This option focuses on the *external impact* of the choices.

| Old Name | New Name | Symbol | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sin | **Hustle** | 🏃 | A term that is often celebrated in "grind culture." By framing "Sin" as "Hustle," the game satirizes the "whatever it takes" mentality of modern work life. |
| Virtue | **Help** | 🤝 | A simple, clear, and positive term. It's about collaboration, community, and altruism. |

**Why it works:** This pairing creates a direct conflict between individual ambition and community well-being, which is a core theme of the game.

## 4. Recommendation

While all three options have merit, I recommend **Option 1: "Shortcut" vs. "Sacrifice."**

This pairing is the most flexible and aligns best with the game's core mechanics. It's less about judging the player's choices and more about describing the nature of those choices. It allows for a wider range of card designs and feels the most timeless.

However, **Option 2: "Sellout" vs. "Self-Care"** is a close second and would be a great choice if the goal is to lean more heavily into direct satire of current trends.

## 5. Implementation

This change would require a simple "find and replace" in the game's documentation and card templates. The core mechanics would not need to be changed.

By making this small but significant change, "Reality Check" can become a more resonant, humorous, and insightful game for its target audience.
